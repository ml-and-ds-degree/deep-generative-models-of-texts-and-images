\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct the one-stochastic-layer, $K=50$ MNIST IWAE experiment of
\citet{burda2016importance}, then evaluate two meaningful extensions: DReG
and progressive-particle DReG.  The reconstruction reaches $87.804$ test nats
after 121 passes.  DReG modestly improves likelihood to $87.480$ nats but not
KID.  Progressive-particle DReG reaches $86.733$ nats, KID $178.08\pm21.76$,
and trains in $14.0$ minutes rather than about $71.5$ minutes for fixed-$K$
DReG.  All numerical comparisons use seed 236; they are evidence for a
practical profile, not a multi-seed superiority claim.
\end{abstract}

\part{Paper Reconstruction}

\section{Original architecture}
For a binarized image $x$, the model samples
$z_{1:K}\sim q_\phi(z\mid x)$ and maximizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_K(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\frac{p_\theta(x,z_k)}{q_\phi(z_k\mid x)}\right].
\end{equation}
We minimize $-\mathcal{L}_{50}$ during training.  Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}
and Table~\ref{tab:reconstruction-setup} give every architecture and
training choice needed to reproduce the selected paper condition.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/architecture.pdf}
\caption{Source-backed architecture diagram.  Both encoder and decoder use
two 200-unit $\tanh$ hidden layers; the posterior is a 50-dimensional diagonal
Gaussian and the decoder is Bernoulli.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\section{Training set-up}
The paper reports NLL in nats and active units.  NLL is estimated here by
$-\mathcal{L}_{5000}$ (lower is better); an active unit has posterior-mean
variance above $0.01$ across examples.  The public MNIST dataset is downloaded
by the project data module; the code and exact commands are in the repository

\part{DReG Improvement}

\section{Training set-up}
DReG is the assignment's meaningful loss/gradient modification: it retains
the generative model and IWAE objective but replaces the encoder's high-
variance gradient estimator with the doubly reparameterized estimator of
\citet{tucker2019doubly}.  The encoder surrogate is
\begin{equation}
-\sum_k \operatorname{sg}(\widetilde w_k)^2\log w_k,
\qquad \widetilde w_k=\frac{w_k}{\sum_j w_j},
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients through the normalized weight.  The
decoder keeps the ordinary IWAE gradient.  Data, architecture, seed,
initialization, $K=50$, batch size 20, Adam settings, 121-pass budget, and
the validation/test procedures remain fixed, so the changed estimator is the
intended experimental variable.

\part{Progressive-Particle Improvement}

\section{Training set-up}
This second improvement keeps DReG but changes the optimisation profile:
batch size $20\rightarrow100$, particles $5\rightarrow10\rightarrow50$, and
cosine learning-rate decay from $3\times10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$.  Validation is
always $K=50$, making checkpoint selection directly comparable with fixed-$K$
DReG.  It is an optimisation extension, not a claim of paper-faithful
training.

KID is the required course metric in addition to the paper's NLL.  It is
computed as Kernel Inception Distance \citep{binkowski2018demystifying} in a

50 subsets of 1,000, and seed 236.  Lower KID means closer feature
distributions.  The reported $\pm$ value is subset standard deviation, not
replication uncertainty; the original paper did not report KID.

\section{Discussion and lessons}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What worked.}  The original architecture and training core
  reproduce the paper's 25 active units.  DReG improves NLL, while the
  progressive profile improves NLL, KID, and elapsed time simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{What did not.}  DReG alone worsened KID in this run.  A
  truncated 121-pass reconstruction does not match the paper's 3,280-pass NLL.
  \item \textbf{What we learned.}  On this small IWAE, early $K=50$ particles
  are expensive before the proposal is useful.  Allocating particles over
  training is more consequential than changing the estimator alone.
  \item \textbf{Limitations.}  All results use one seed.  The fixed-DReG
  duration comes from interrupted-run artifacts, whereas progressive DReG is
  one uninterrupted run.  Seeds 237 and 238, identical $\mathcal{L}_{5000}$
  evaluation, KID, and a preregistered non-inferiority tolerance are needed
  before claiming reliable superiority.
\end{itemize}
